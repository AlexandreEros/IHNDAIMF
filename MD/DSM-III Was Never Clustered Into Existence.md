# DSM-III was never clustered into existence

**DSM-III's categories were not derived by factor analysis, cluster analysis, or any multivariate method — they were produced by committee consensus and voting, with kappa statistics applied only after the fact to measure interrater agreement.** This single historical fact reframes every serious argument against the manual: the "statistical methodology" of DSM-III is largely a myth. The authoritative nosology of modern psychiatry was built on the neo-Kraepelinian intuitions of roughly a dozen clinicians at Washington University and New York State Psychiatric Institute, formalized into polythetic criteria, and then defended with a rhetorical move — *reliability is a necessary condition for validity* — that was never followed up by any actual validity test. The manifesto's argument that clustering methods were inappropriate for psychiatric symptoms is therefore even stronger than it appears: clustering methods were not merely misapplied, they were **assumed in the format of the criteria without ever being run**. Every subsequent empirical challenge — comorbidity rates of 45–66%, 636,120 ways to qualify for PTSD, [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260750182_636120_Ways_to_Have_Posttraumatic_Stress_Disorder) a single *p*-factor better-fitting than eleven distinct disorders, cross-disorder genetic correlations of 0.43–0.68 — is best understood not as falsifying a carefully derived taxonomy but as exposing the epistemic emptiness of a taxonomy that was never derived at all.

## The statistical method that wasn't

The historical record, most thoroughly documented in Hannah Decker's *The Making of DSM-III* (Oxford, 2013) and in Davies's 2016 *Anthropology and Medicine* interviews, is unambiguous. Spitzer himself described the process: "**We simply discussed things until we were comfortable with it… It was done by consensus of experts, which would now be considered a very trivial approach, but that's the best we could do then.**" [Taylor & Francis Online](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13648470.2016.1226684) The Feighner criteria (1972) — the direct ancestors of DSM-III — were drafted by a Washington University discussion group [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20008944/) starting from prior clinical papers like Cassidy et al.'s depression symptom list and modifying them through conversation. The famous 6-of-10 threshold for depression was chosen, in group member Muñoz's words, "**because it sounded about right.**" Kendler, Aggen and Neale (*JAMA Psychiatry* 2013) confirm: "Clinical judgment and not psychometrics guided the development of these criteria." [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3800168/)

The only statistic that played a central role was **Cohen's/Fleiss's kappa**, operationalized in Spitzer & Fleiss's 1974 *British Journal of Psychiatry* re-analysis. That paper's rhetorical move is the pivot of the entire DSM enterprise: "*A necessary constraint on the validity of a system is its reliability. There is no guarantee that a reliable system is valid, but assuredly an unreliable system must be invalid.*" [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/abs/reanalysis-of-the-reliability-of-psychiatric-diagnosis/FEBECBB1AEB68DA969F897333B8003E3) Having defined pre-1974 diagnosis as unacceptably unreliable, Spitzer created the crisis DSM-III would solve. [Academyanalyticarts](https://academyanalyticarts.org/kirk-myth-reliability-dsm) The DSM-III field trials (Spitzer, Forman, Nee, *AJP* 1979) then reported Axis I kappa of 0.78 — but the design was methodologically favorable: joint interviews (where two raters share the same observation stream inflate agreement), volunteer sympathetic clinicians, and aggregated statistics hiding individual-diagnosis kappas below 0.5. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/443467/) Chmielewski, Clark, Bagby & Watson (*Journal of Abnormal Psychology* 2015) showed that under realistic test-retest independent interviews, mean DSM-IV kappa was ~0.47 — fair, not good. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4573819/) Kraemer et al. (2012) quietly relaxed what would count as "acceptable" kappa for DSM-5 rather than confront these numbers. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264796782_Reliability_in_Psychiatric_Diagnosis_with_the_DSM_Old_Wine_in_New_Barrels)

The embedded assumptions were never tested, only assumed: that disorders are **discrete categorical kinds** (no taxometric check); that symptoms are **exchangeable compensatory indicators** of those kinds (the 5-of-9 polythetic rule assumes equal weighting); that **interrater reliability is a precondition for validity** rather than a measure of rule-application agreement; and that **committee consensus is epistemically equivalent to empirical discovery**. The Robins-Guze (1970) validator framework — clinical description, laboratory study, delimitation, follow-up, family study — was offered as the validation program, but phases 2-5 were never completed for most disorders.

## Three converging formal critiques

Three lines of formal argument from recognized authorities independently converge on rejecting DSM validity.

**Kendler's mechanistic property cluster (MPC) framework** (Kendler, Zachar & Craver, *Psychological Medicine* 2011, 41:1143–1150) systematically rules out the four candidate ontologies of psychiatric disorder: essentialist natural kinds [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=What+kinds+of+things+are+psychiatric+disorders?&amp=&author=K.+S.+Kendler&amp=&author=P.+Zachar&amp=&author=C.+Craver&amp=&publication_year=2011&amp=&pages=1143-1150&amp=&doi=10.1017/S0033291710001844) (falsified by polygenicity and heterogeneity), social constructs (falsified by cross-cultural recurrence), **practical kinds** (the DSM's implicit stance, rejected as scientifically unambitious because it "does not require that diagnoses be grounded in shared causal processes"), and MPC kinds (endorsed). [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=What+kinds+of+things+are+psychiatric+disorders?&amp=&author=K.+S.+Kendler&amp=&author=P.+Zachar&amp=&author=C.+Craver&amp=&publication_year=2011&amp=&pages=1143-1150&amp=&doi=10.1017/S0033291710001844) Under MPC, disorders are "fuzzy sets defined by mechanisms at multiple levels… populations with central paradigmatic and more marginal members." [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=What+kinds+of+things+are+psychiatric+disorders?&amp=&author=K.+S.+Kendler&amp=&author=P.+Zachar&amp=&author=C.+Craver&amp=&publication_year=2011&amp=&pages=1143-1150&amp=&doi=10.1017/S0033291710001844) Because mechanisms — not surface features — define the kinds, **symptom-level clustering cannot in principle recover valid categories**. Kendler's 2012 *Molecular Psychiatry* paper extends this with "dappled" causation: causes are distributed in irregular patches across biological, psychological, and social levels, so any flat symptom-level procedure is mechanism-blind.

**Hyman's reification critique** (*Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* 2010, 6:155–179) introduces the most quotable formulation: DSM created "an **unintended epistemic prison that was palpably impeding scientific progress**." [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/HYMTDO-3) Hyman documents that researchers proceeded "as if [DSM disorders] were **natural kinds that would map onto the human genome**." [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/HYMTDO-3) They do not. Two patients with identical MDD diagnoses can share only one of nine criteria. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/HYMTDO-3) His structural argument has five steps: reliability ≠ validity; polythetic criteria breed within-category heterogeneity; [Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev.clinpsy.3.022806.091532) comorbidity of ~50% suggests miscarved boundaries; family and genetic data disrespect DSM categories; and FDA/grant/journal lock-in makes the reification self-sustaining.

**Insel's NIMH announcement** (Director's Blog, 29 April 2013) was the institutional rupture. "DSM has been described as a 'Bible' for the field, **it is, at best, a dictionary**… **The weakness is its lack of validity.** Unlike our definitions of ischemic heart disease, lymphoma, or AIDS, DSM diagnoses are based on a consensus about clusters of clinical symptoms, not any objective laboratory measure." [Psychrights](https://psychrights.org/2013/130429NIMHTransformingDiagnosis.htm) Crucially: "**We cannot succeed if we use DSM categories as the 'gold standard'… Imagine deciding that EKGs were not useful because many patients with chest pain did not have EKG changes.**" [Psychrights](https://psychrights.org/2013/130429NIMHTransformingDiagnosis.htm) The RDoC framework (Insel et al., *AJP* 2010; Cuthbert & Insel, *BMC Medicine* 2013) operationalizes this into dimensional, mechanism-based units of analysis. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1741-7015-11-126) The convergence with Kendler and Hyman is explicit: RDoC cites both.

## The empirical wreckage

The numerical evidence against DSM categorical validity has reached the point of absurdity.

**Comorbidity runs on a rule of 50%**. The National Comorbidity Survey Replication (Kessler et al., *Archives of General Psychiatry* 2005) [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2847357/) found 45% of people with any 12-month DSM-IV disorder met criteria for two or more. The Dunedin birth-cohort data analyzed by Caspi & Moffitt (*AJP* 2018) show the pattern is fractal: of those meeting criteria for one disorder, 66% met criteria for a second; of those with two, 53% met criteria for a third; of those with three, 41% met criteria for a fourth. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29621902/) **Diagnostic stability collapses over time**: Bromet et al. (*AJP* 2011) followed first-admission psychosis for ten years and found **50.7% of patients changed diagnostic category**; [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3589618/) Suárez-Pinilla et al. reported only 47.7% stability at 21 years in the PAFIP cohort, with 80% having changed by year 10. [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/abs/longterm-diagnostic-stability-predictors-of-diagnostic-change-and-time-until-diagnostic-change-of-firstepisode-psychosis-a-21year-followup-study/2D7A6A5775A2AEA714260DC85BB4A19D)

**Within-category heterogeneity explodes combinatorially**. Fried & Nesse (*Journal of Affective Disorders* 2015) analyzed STAR*D (n=3,703) [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032714006326) and found **1,030 unique symptom profiles** for DSM-defined MDD; [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S027273582100043X) 83.9% of profiles were endorsed by five or fewer participants. [Arizona State University](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/depression-is-not-a-consistent-syndrome-an-investigation-of-uniqu/) With decompounded sleep/appetite/psychomotor items, MDD admits **10,377 qualifying combinations**, [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4397113/) [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278584620304498) rising to **341,737 with the melancholic specifier**. [Eiko Fried +2](https://eiko-fried.com/10377-ways-for-major-depression-but-341737-ways-for-melancholia/) Galatzer-Levy & Bryant's *Perspectives on Psychological Science* (2013) paper — titled "**636,120 Ways to Have Posttraumatic Stress Disorder**" — documents that DSM-5 expanded PTSD's polythetic combinatorics eightfold from DSM-IV. [Sage Journals +4](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1745691613504115) Olbert, Gala & Tupler (*Journal of Abnormal Psychology* 2014) proved formally that in most DSM categories, **two patients with the same diagnosis can share zero symptoms**. [PubMed +2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24886017/)

**Treatment response betrays categorical specificity in both directions**. Within MDD, STAR*D's (*AJP* 2006) sequential remission rates were 36.8%, 30.6%, 13.7%, 13.0% across steps; [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11920-007-0061-3) Pigott et al.'s 2023 *BMJ* reanalysis found the true cumulative remission was ~35%, not the advertised 67%. [Psychiatry Times](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/star-d-s-cumulative-remission-rate-and-why-it-still-matters) No active antidepressant beat any other. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/070674371005500303) CATIE (Lieberman et al., *NEJM* 2005) found 74% of schizophrenia patients discontinued their assigned antipsychotic within 18 months, [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6482974/) [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5935444/) with no advantage for newer atypicals. [Psychiatry Online](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2011.11010039) Across categories, **the same drugs work for ostensibly distinct disorders**: SSRIs are first-line for MDD, GAD, panic, OCD, PTSD, social anxiety, and PMDD; lithium reduces suicidality across diagnostic boundaries; Barlow's Unified Protocol (*JAMA Psychiatry* 2017) matched diagnosis-specific CBT across GAD, SAD, OCD, and panic. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wps.20748) Cipriani et al.'s 522-trial network meta-analysis (*Lancet* 2018) found all 21 antidepressants modestly superior to placebo (SMD 0.30) with no meaningful specificity. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477251/) [University of Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/comparative-efficacy-and-acceptability-of-21-antidepressant-drugs/)

**Genetics refuses to respect DSM boundaries**. The Cross-Disorder Psychiatric Genomics Consortium (Lee et al., *Nature Genetics* 2013) estimated SNP-based genetic correlations [The Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(12)62129-1/abstract) [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?hl=en&volume=381&publication_year=2013&pages=1371-1379&journal=Lancet&author=Cross-Disorder+Group+of+the+Psychiatric+Genomics+Consortium&title=Identification+of+risk+loci+with+shared+effects+on+five+major+psychiatric+disorders:+A+genome-wide+analysis) of **0.68 between schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, 0.47 bipolar-MDD, 0.43 schizophrenia-MDD**. The Brainstorm Consortium (*Science* 2018), analyzing 265,218 patients across 25 brain disorders, found psychiatric disorders share common-variant risk promiscuously while neurological disorders are genetically distinct — **psychiatric DSM categories do not cut at genetic joints**. [King's College London](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/analysis-of-shared-heritability-in-common-disorders-of-the-brain/) [Hal](https://cea.hal.science/cea-01870483v1/document) Selzam et al. (*Translational Psychiatry* 2018) extracted a **polygenic p-factor explaining 22-57% of variance** across methods, [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-018-0217-4) paralleling Caspi et al.'s (*Clinical Psychological Science* 2014) behavioral p-factor that fit Dunedin data better than three-factor or category models. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25360393/) [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209412/) Kendler's own 1996 twin work established that GAD and MDD share essentially identical genetic liabilities.

## The dimensional alternative and its mathematical teeth

The Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology (Kotov et al., *Journal of Abnormal Psychology* 2017, 126:454–477) is the consensus document of 40 leading nosologists specifying what a post-DSM taxonomy looks like: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28333488/) [Unt](https://class.unt.edu/hitop/images/hitop.unt.edu/files/kotov_et_al_2017_-_hitop.pdf) six empirical spectra (internalizing, thought disorder, disinhibited externalizing, antagonistic externalizing, detachment, somatoform) organized into three superspectra under a general *p*-factor, with syndromes below and symptom components at the bottom. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6859953/) Its methods — confirmatory factor analysis, bifactor modeling, Goldberg's bass-ackwards procedure, latent-class-vs-latent-trait comparisons across samples up to 43,093 participants — represent what DSM-III could have done in 1980 and did not. [APA](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/abn-abn0000258.pdf) [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315591130_The_Hierarchical_Taxonomy_of_Psychopathology_HiTOP_A_Dimensional_Alternative_to_Traditional_Nosologies) Watson et al. (*World Psychiatry* 2022) state the verdict plainly: "**not a single mental disorder has been established in the scientific literature as a discrete categorical entity**." [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_Taxonomy_of_Psychopathology)

The formal mathematical case against DSM's categorical format runs through **MacCallum, Zhang, Preacher & Rucker** (*Psychological Methods* 2002), whose worked examples show [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-the-practice-of-dichotomization-of-quantitative-Maccallum-Zhang/67646d3def337c40bbfa2476f0f1ff785ae18242) dichotomization of a continuous variable with true correlation r = .30 reduces the observed correlation to ~.21, discarding more than half the explained variance. [Quantpsy](https://quantpsy.org/pubs/maccallum_zhang_preacher_rucker_2002.pdf) Applied to DSM's 5-of-9 MDD threshold or 6-of-20 PTSD threshold, this is a rigorous demonstration that the polythetic cutoff **destroys information rather than compressing it usefully**. Haslam, McGrath, Viechtbauer & Kuppens's (*Psychological Medicine* 2020) meta-analysis of 317 taxometric findings using the Comparative Curve Fit Index found dimensional models outperforming taxonic models **5:1**, with zero content domains aggregating as taxonic [Maastricht University](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/dimensions-over-categories-a-meta-analysis-of-taxometric-research/) — only six variables (alcohol dependence, IED, problem gambling, autism, suicide risk, pedophilia) showed replicated taxonic signatures. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32493520/)

Borsboom's network theory (*World Psychiatry* 2017, 16:5–13) supplies the alternative ontology: [Denny Borsboom](https://dennyborsboom.com/publications/) **disorders are not latent entities causing symptoms but alternative stable states of causally connected symptom networks**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28127906/) When connectivity exceeds a threshold, symptom activation becomes self-sustaining. [PubMed +2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23537483/) This dispenses with the latent-variable assumption underlying both categorical and dimensional models that treat symptoms as indicators of some hidden construct.

## Brown clustering: a defensible analogy, but not the load-bearing one

No prior psychiatric paper uses Brown clustering as an analogy for DSM failure — searches across PubMed, PMC, ACL Anthology, and computational psychiatry venues return nothing. The analogy is **defensible if handled carefully, cringe if handled casually**. Its strength is that Brown clustering's known failure mode is structurally identical to the DSM problem: it brilliantly groups `{Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, ...}` (shallow distributional near-synonyms) but produces clusters like `{great, big, vast, sudden, mere, sheer, gigantic, lifelong, scant, colossal}` — real in co-occurrence statistics, incoherent in semantic mechanism. [Simonsuster](https://simonsuster.github.io/talks/Brown_RUG.pdf) That mirrors DSM: insomnia, fatigue, and low mood co-occur in clinic regardless of whether the generative mechanism is sleep apnea, grief, SSRI withdrawal, or hypothyroidism. A greedy agglomerative mutual-information algorithm [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/10190/10049) with no theoretical guarantees [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_clustering) is exactly the right formal cousin of a committee-consensus symptom-grouping procedure.

The risks are that computational psychiatry audiences (Huys, Gillan, Stephan, Friston circles) may find Brown clustering arbitrary — it's a 1992 algorithm long superseded by word2vec/GloVe/BERT — and read its invocation as namedropping from someone who hasn't read the field's own critiques. The manifesto should **frame Brown clustering as a vivid illustrative cousin, not the primary frame**, and embed it inside the field's canonical vocabulary: Borsboom, Mellenbergh & van Heerden (*Psychological Review* 2004) on validity requiring "(a) the attribute exists and (b) variations in the attribute causally produce variation in the measurement outcomes"; [Dennyborsboom](https://dennyborsboom.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/borsboomvalidity2004.pdf) Michell's charge that psychometrics is "pathological science" for assuming measurability rather than demonstrating it (*Theory & Psychology* 2000; *Measurement* 2008); and the computational-psychiatry consensus — Huys, Maia & Frank (*Nature Neuroscience* 2016); [Ethz](https://www.tnu.ethz.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/documents/Publications/2016/2016_Huys_Maia_Frank.pdf) [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Computational-psychiatry-as-a-bridge-from-to-Huys-Maia/42abde7800c25b90ec1bb9c88dfbe2ab37e5ae16) Stephan et al. (*Lancet Psychiatry* 2016); Wise, Robinson & Gillan (*Biol Psychiatry* 2023) — that the field has already abandoned DSM categories as research endpoints.

Stronger or complementary computational analogies include **phlogiston** (reliable descriptive compression, zero mechanism), **Ptolemaic epicycles** (DSM specifiers and NOS categories as structural epicycles), **k-means on non-convex manifolds** (the algorithm always returns clusters whether or not cluster structure exists), and **overfitting to noise** (Wiecki, Poland & Frank, *Clinical Psychological Science* 2015). The most intellectually robust framing combines them: DSM categories are to psychiatric mechanisms what Brown clusters are to semantic concepts, what phlogiston is to oxidation, and what epicycles are to gravity — **reliable surface compressions of phenomena whose generative structure lies elsewhere**.

## Conclusion: the unclaimed information-theoretic argument

The strongest novel contribution a manifesto can make is not another rehearsal of the empirical wreckage but a **formal information-theoretic framing that the literature has not yet claimed**. Three gaps are open. First, **no one has formalized DSM categorization as a failed minimum description length (MDL) problem**: a valid categorical compression should produce shorter descriptions than the raw symptom vector while preserving predictive power over outcomes and treatment response. DSM labels manifestly fail both conditions — 1,030 MDD profiles cannot be losslessly compressed to one symbol, [ScienceDirect +3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S027273582100043X) and the symbol does not predict SSRI response better than the vector. Second, **the "encoding loss chain"** — lived experience → patient's metaphor → clinician's interpretation → binary criterion checkbox — is extensively described qualitatively (Kleinman's illness-vs-disease distinction; interview reliability literature) but has not been modeled as a cascade of information-destroying channels with quantifiable per-stage entropy loss. Third, **the disanalogy with linguistic corpora is sharp and unexploited**: Brown clustering works for language because [Sean-chester](http://sean-chester.github.io/assets/preprints/generalised-brown.pdf) speakers share a generative grammar and stable distributional semantics; psychiatric symptoms lack this shared generative structure because the same surface symptom arises from mechanistically unrelated causal networks (multiple realizability, in Borsboom et al.'s 2019 *BBS* formulation). The DSM assumed symptoms were like words in a corpus. They are not. The manifesto's deepest claim — that clustering methods failed because psychiatric symptoms lack shared semantic structure — is both correct and, as yet, unmade in the formal literature. That is the space to occupy.
