# Ibogaine and 9-Methyl-β-Carboline (9-Me-BC) as Candidate Interventions for D2 Receptor Recovery after Chronic Stimulant Use

## Integration Note

The following two sections are drafted to slot into an existing scientific document on stimulant-induced D2/D3 downregulation that already covers GASP-1 degradation, ΔFosB/HDAC1 transcriptional repression, DRD2 promoter epigenetic silencing, absent spontaneous recovery on PET at 9 months, exercise (Robertson 2016), preclinical HDAC inhibitors, AAV-DRD2/CRISPRa gene therapy, and BBB-delivery technology. Consistent with that document's style, claims are mechanism-anchored, distinguishing preclinical from human evidence, and avoiding speculative framing. A central caveat applies to both compounds: **neither has been directly demonstrated to upregulate striatal D2/D3 receptor density in humans by PET imaging**, and for both, the case for D2 recovery must be constructed from indirect, upstream mechanisms (neurotrophic induction, restoration of dopaminergic tone, reversal of transcriptional repression surrogates) rather than direct receptor-level endpoints.

## Ibogaine

### 1. Mechanisms Relevant to D2R Recovery and Dopaminergic Neuroprotection

**No direct D2/D3 receptor affinity.** Radioligand binding surveys consistently show that ibogaine has no meaningful affinity for dopamine D1, D2, D3, or D4 receptors at concentrations ≤100 μM; compiled Ki values place D3 at ~70,000 nM, [Ibogaworld](https://www.ibogaworld.com/ibogainebinding-profile/) well above achievable brain concentrations [[Sweetnam et al., 1995, Psychopharmacology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02245936); [ScienceDirect, Iboga topic summary citing Deecher 1992, Staley 1996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/iboga)]. Any D2R-related effect of ibogaine is therefore necessarily indirect, mediated through neurotrophic, transcriptional, or circuit-level changes rather than orthosteric action at DRD2.

**GDNF induction in the VTA — the principal candidate mechanism.** The strongest and most replicated dopamine-relevant mechanism is induction of glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) in midbrain dopaminergic structures. He et al. demonstrated in Journal of Neuroscience that systemic ibogaine increased GDNF mRNA in a rat midbrain region containing the VTA, upregulated phosphorylation of the GDNF receptor RET and downstream ERK1 in SH-SY5Y dopaminergic cells, and that microinjection of ibogaine directly into the VTA (but not the substantia nigra) was sufficient to reduce ethanol self-administration [Journal of Neuroscience](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/25/3/619) [[He et al., 2005, J Neurosci 25:619–628](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/25/3/619); [PMC1193648](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1193648/)]. A subsequent paper from the same group identified an autoregulatory positive-feedback loop: an initial GDNF pulse induces further GDNF expression through its own signaling cascade, producing a self-sustaining cycle that can persist for weeks beyond clearance of the parent drug and its metabolite noribogaine [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1744383/full) [[He & Ron, 2006, FASEB J 20:E1820–E1827, doi:10.1096/fj.06-6394fje](https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1096/fj.06-6394fje)]. Marton et al. extended this to multiple neurotrophic factors, showing that 40 mg/kg ibogaine in rats selectively upregulated GDNF transcripts and mature GDNF protein in the VTA and substantia nigra, with broader BDNF and NGF increases across PFC, NAcc, SN, and VTA at 24 h [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/60c73f20337d6c5e99e264df) [[Marton et al., 2019, Front Pharmacol, doi:10.3389/fphar.2019.00193](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2019.00193/full); [PubMed 30890941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30890941/)]. Mechanistic relevance to D2 recovery is that GDNF, via GFRα1/RET signaling, promotes survival of VTA/SNc dopaminergic neurons, [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1744383/full) phosphorylation of tyrosine hydroxylase, enhanced dopaminergic neuron excitability through A-type K⁺ channel modulation, and dendritic/synaptic remodeling in mesocorticolimbic circuits [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1744383/full) — the same circuits in which chronic stimulant use produces hypodopaminergic tone that drives compensatory D2/D3 downregulation [[He et al., 2005](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/25/3/619); [Frontiers in Pharmacology narrative review 2025, doi:10.3389/fphar.2025.1744383](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1744383/full)].

**σ2 receptor agonism.** Ibogaine is a moderately selective σ2 ligand (Ki ≈ 201 nM at σ2 versus 8,554 nM at σ1; ~43-fold σ2 selectivity) [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001429999500247I) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7556375/) [[Bowen et al., 1995, Eur J Pharmacol 279:R1–R3, PubMed 7556375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7556375/); [Floresta et al., 2019, Int J Mol Sci 20:488, doi:10.3390/ijms20030488](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/20/3/488)]. σ2 activation elicits transient release of Ca²⁺ from endoplasmic reticulum stores [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001429999500247I) (thapsigargin-sensitive, extracellular Ca²⁺-independent), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001429999500247I) and σ2 receptors have been implicated in modulating neuronal viability and presynaptic dopamine release in striatum. Sershen et al. showed that 1 μM ibogaine inhibited both NMDA- and (+)-pentazocine–evoked striatal [³H]DA release by 31% and 48% respectively, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8722755/) consistent with σ2 participation in ibogaine's dopaminergic modulation [[Sershen et al., 1996, PubMed 8722755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8722755/)]. σ2/TMEM97 engagement is not a demonstrated driver of D2R resynthesis, but it is part of the mechanistic package usually invoked when explaining ibogaine's neurobiological distinctiveness from classical 5-HT2A psychedelics [[Floresta et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6386901/)].

**DAT/SERT pharmacology and pharmacochaperone activity.** Ibogaine is an atypical, noncompetitive inhibitor of SERT (Ki ~0.5 μM) and DAT (Ki ~2 μM) that stabilizes the inward-facing conformation of the transporters — a mechanism distinct from every other known SERT inhibitor, which bind the outward-facing state [[Bulling et al., 2012, J Biol Chem 287:18524–18534, PubMed 22451652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22451652/); [PMC3365767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3365767/)]. This unusual conformational trapping is the basis for a pharmacochaperoning action: ibogaine and noribogaine rescue folding-deficient DAT and SERT mutants [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33860180/) from ER retention and restore surface expression and transport function, first described in the context of DAT deficiency syndrome mutations [[Bhat et al., 2021, ACS Pharmacol Transl Sci, doi:10.1021/acsptsci.0c00102, PubMed 33860180](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33860180/); [bioRxiv 2020.07.14.202325](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.07.14.202325v1.full)]. For the stimulant-recovery context this is notable because chronic methamphetamine and cocaine exposure produce DAT misfolding, mislocalization, and degradation analogous to (though not identical to) the GASP-1/DRD2 degradation pathway. Ibogaine also has low-affinity interaction with VMAT2 ([¹²⁵I]tetrabenazine site), and noribogaine has been reported to produce dual VMAT2 and SERT inhibition with comparable potency [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369174923_IUPHAR_-_invited_review_-_Ibogaine_-_A_legacy_within_the_current_renaissance_of_psychedelic_therapy) [[Mash 2023, IUPHAR invited review, Pharmacol Res 190:106620, doi:10.1016/j.phrs.2022.106620](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36907284/)].

**Summary on D2R specifically.** There is no direct evidence that ibogaine upregulates DRD2 transcription, reverses ΔFosB/HDAC1-mediated DRD2 repression, or increases striatal D2/D3 receptor density in any species. The mechanistic rationale for including ibogaine in a discussion of D2 recovery rests entirely on (1) VTA GDNF induction with autoregulatory persistence, which should rebuild dopaminergic tone whose chronic deficit underlies compensatory D2 downregulation, and (2) pharmacochaperone rescue of mis-trafficked monoamine transporters. These mechanisms would be predicted to relieve, rather than directly reverse, transcriptional silencing at the DRD2 locus.

### 2. Best Human Clinical Evidence Relevant to Stimulant Use Disorder

Controlled human efficacy data for stimulant use disorder remain limited, and every existing human dataset has significant methodological constraints.

**Mash/St. Kitts open-label series (1996–2003).** Conducted at Healing Visions Institute [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5996271/) under government approval in St. Kitts, this is the largest prospective inpatient dataset combining opioid- and cocaine-dependent patients (N = 191 in the published analysis). A 12-day inpatient protocol [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2018.00529/full) assessed safety, pharmacokinetics, craving, and mood; [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2018.00529/full) multi-dimensional craving questionnaires [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29922156/) demonstrated reductions in both heroin and cocaine craving, and no significant adverse events were reported at effective doses [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29922156/) in medically screened participants [[Mash, Duque, Page & Allen-Ferdinand, 2018, Front Pharmacol 9:529, doi:10.3389/fphar.2018.00529, PubMed 29922156](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29922156/); [PMC5996271](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5996271/)]. These are retrospective chart analyses of an open-label series, not a controlled efficacy trial.

**Schenberg et al., Brazil.** The most stimulant-specific human dataset comes from Brazil, where ibogaine is unregulated (not banned, not scheduled). A retrospective analysis of 75 patients (alcohol, cannabis, cocaine and crack users; 72% polydrug) treated under medical supervision with ibogaine HCl combined with psychotherapy reported 61% abstinence at follow-up, median abstinence of 5.5 months after a single treatment and 8.4 months after multiple treatments, and no serious adverse reactions or fatalities in the cohort [[Schenberg et al., 2014, J Psychopharmacol 28:993–1000, doi:10.1177/0269881114552713, PubMed 25271214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25271214/)]. A follow-up qualitative study corroborated improvements in quality of life even in partial responders [[Schenberg et al., 2017, J Psychedelic Stud 1:10–19](https://akjournals.com/view/journals/2054/1/1/article-p10.xml)]. A randomized placebo-controlled phase IIb trial in 80 cocaine/crack users led by Negrão at USP/São Paulo has been in preparation but has not published primary endpoints [[MAPS, 2021 review of Brazilian ibogaine research](https://maps.org/news/bulletin/the-case-of-brazil-after-ayahuasca-ibogaine-opens-up-opportunity-for-first-class-research-in-the-global-south/)].

**Noller et al., New Zealand.** A 12-month observational study of opioid-dependent patients treated in New Zealand (where ibogaine is scheduled as a non-approved medicine and can be prescribed), reporting sustained reductions in opioid use and ASI scores; stimulant use disorder was not the primary indication [[Noller, Frampton & Yazar-Klosinski, 2018, Am J Drug Alcohol Abuse 44:37–46, doi:10.1080/00952990.2017.1310218](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00952990.2017.1310218)].

**Glue et al., noribogaine RCT.** The only double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of an iboga alkaloid to date evaluated single ascending doses (60/120/180 mg) of noribogaine in 27 methadone-maintained opioid-dependent patients in New Zealand. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/294138827_Ascending_Single-Dose_Double-Blind_Placebo-Controlled_Safety_Study_of_Noribogaine_in_Opioid-Dependent_Patients) It assessed safety, pharmacokinetics (t½ 24–30 h, dose-linear Cmax/AUC), and QTc, and did not detect a reduction in opioid withdrawal symptoms; [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/294138827_Ascending_Single-Dose_Double-Blind_Placebo-Controlled_Safety_Study_of_Noribogaine_in_Opioid-Dependent_Patients) it was not powered for efficacy and did not enroll stimulant users [[Glue et al., 2016, Clin Pharmacol Drug Dev 5:460–468, doi:10.1002/cpdd.254, PubMed 26861078](https://accp1.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cpdd.254)].

**Stanford veterans trial (MISTIC).** The Stanford magnesium–ibogaine trial referenced in the task description was published in *Nature Medicine* (not *Psychological Medicine*) by Cherian, Keynan, Anker et al. It is a prospective open-label observational study of 30 male U.S. Special Operations Forces veterans with predominantly mild TBI, comorbid PTSD (n = 23), MDD (n = 15), anxiety (n = 14), and alcohol use disorder (n = 15) or other SUD (n = 6). Participants enrolled independently at Ambio Life Sciences in Mexico [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032725021640) for a single magnesium–ibogaine session; pre- and post-treatment assessments were conducted at Stanford. At one month, WHODAS disability scores fell from 30.2 to 5.1, PTSD and depression scores fell by approximately 88% and 87% respectively, and no serious cardiac events were reported [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/drug-discovery/news/african-psychedelic-ibogaine-reduces-veterans-brain-injury-symptoms-in-small-trial-382964) [[Cherian et al., 2024, Nat Med 30:373–381, doi:10.1038/s41591-023-02705-w, PubMed 38182784](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38182784/); [nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02705-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02705-w)]. A follow-on neurophysiology paper described sustained shifts in peak alpha frequency [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032725021640) and cortical complexity [[Lissemore et al., 2025, Nat Mental Health 3:918–931](https://www.usmedicine.com/clinical-topics/tbi/magnesium-ibogaine-therapy-could-offer-treatment-option-for-tbi/)]. The MISTIC cohort is not a stimulant-use-disorder study, although 15/30 met AUD criteria and 6/30 had other SUDs; the stimulant-specific signal must therefore be extrapolated.

**Overall evidence grade for stimulant use disorder.** The available human evidence is dominated by open-label case series and retrospective cohorts; there are no completed randomized placebo-controlled efficacy trials of ibogaine in cocaine, methamphetamine, or amphetamine-type stimulant use disorder [[Rodríguez et al., 2022, systematic review, J Subst Abuse Treat, doi:10.1016/j.jsat.2021.108717, PubMed 35012793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35012793/)].

### 3. Cardiac Safety: QTc Prolongation and Its Management

The dominant safety signal is QTc prolongation through hERG/KCNH2 (IKr) block, with risk of Torsades de Pointes. In vitro, ibogaine blocks hERG currents with IC50 ≈ 3.9 μM — within the therapeutic concentration range [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22458604/) — and noribogaine similarly inhibits hERG [[Koenig, Kovar, Boehm, Sandtner & Hilber, 2014, Addict Biol 19:237–239, PubMed 22458604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22458604/); [Koenig & Hilber, 2015, Molecules 20:2208–2228, doi:10.3390/molecules20022208](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/20/2/2208)]. Published case reports describe QTc values from minor prolongation (~480 ms) to extreme values (>700 ms, including one 730 ms recovery over 7 days after a 65–70 mg/kg exposure), with Torsades de Pointes documented in multiple instances [[Steinberg & Deyell, 2018, J Arrhythm, doi:10.1002/joa3.12061](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/joa3.12061); [Koenig & Hilber, 2015](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/20/2/2208)]. In the best-controlled PK/PD study, Knuijver et al. gave 10 mg/kg ibogaine HCl to 14 opioid-use-disorder patients in the Netherlands and modeled a sigmoid Emax relationship between ibogaine plasma concentration and QTc; maximum QTc prolongation averaged ~95 ms, with approximately half of subjects exceeding QTc 500 ms during observation — a threshold conventionally considered a major arrhythmia risk [[Knuijver et al., 2024, J Psychopharmacol 38:481–488, doi:10.1177/02698811241237873, PubMed 38519421](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11102648/)]. Ibogaine clearance was strongly correlated with CYP2D6 activity score (p < 0.001), exposing poor metabolizers to markedly higher and more sustained plasma levels [[Knuijver et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11102648/)].

**Contemporary risk-management protocols.** Current ibogaine clinical programs manage this risk through (1) pre-treatment cardiac screening (12-lead ECG, echocardiogram, electrolytes) with exclusion of QTc >450 ms (men) or >470 ms (women), structural heart disease, or history of syncope; (2) correction of potassium and magnesium (serum K 3.5–5.0 mmol/L); (3) CYP2D6 genotyping or phenotyping and exclusion or dose-adjustment in poor metabolizers and in patients on CYP2D6 inhibitors or QTc-prolonging drugs; (4) continuous telemetry for ≥24–48 h post-dose given noribogaine's 28–49 h half-life and delayed QTc peak; and (5) pre- and post-treatment magnesium loading, which is the distinguishing feature of the Stanford/Ambio MISTIC protocol and was associated with no reported cardiac symptoms at one month in the 30 treated veterans [[Cherian et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02705-w); Global Ibogaine Therapy Alliance Clinical Guidelines, cited in [Rodríguez et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35012793/)]. Structural analogues with reduced hERG liability and reduced hallucinogenic activity — 18-methoxycoronaridine (18-MC) and tabernanthalog [Jsatjournal](https://www.jsatjournal.com/article/S0740-5472(21)00443-8/fulltext) — are in preclinical and early clinical development as alternatives [[Cameron et al., 2020, Nature 589:474–479, cited in Rodríguez et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35012793/)].

### 4. Regulatory Status

Ibogaine remains a Schedule I substance in the United States (since 1970), and it is the only psychedelic compound named specifically in an April 18, 2026 Executive Order directing federal agencies to accelerate psychedelic research and access pathways [[White House Executive Order, April 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/04/accelerating-medical-treatments-for-serious-mental-illness/); [CNN, April 18, 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/18/politics/trump-signs-executive-order-urging-more-research-into-psychedelic-ibogaine)]. Key regulatory points for the scientific reader:

- **No FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation has been granted for ibogaine itself** as of this writing. The Executive Order and associated Priority Review Voucher mechanism reference psychedelic products that *have received* Breakthrough designations (psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression; LSD/MM120 for generalized anxiety disorder); ibogaine does not currently hold this designation [[BioSpace, April 22, 2026](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/trumps-psychedelics-push-a-mixed-blessing-for-neuropsychiatric-companies-patients); [High Times, 2026](https://hightimes.com/news/politics/it-took-a-text-from-joe-rogan-now-psychedelics-are-a-federal-research-priority/)].
- **First US IND cleared April 2026.** At the Executive Order signing, FDA Commissioner Makary announced the first Investigational New Drug clearance for an ibogaine-class compound; atai subsidiary DemeRx subsequently confirmed the IND covers DMX-1001, an oral noribogaine derivative targeting alcohol use disorder [DemeRx](https://www.demerx.com/the-science/) [[BioSpace, April 22, 2026](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/trumps-psychedelics-push-a-mixed-blessing-for-neuropsychiatric-companies-patients)]. DemeRx/atai had previously obtained UK MHRA clearance for a Phase I/IIa ibogaine trial in opioid use disorder (30 healthy recreational users + 80 opioid-dependent patients) at [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/ibogaine-demerx-atai-mhra-clinical-trial/) MAC Clinical Research, Manchester [[Clinical Trials Arena, 2021](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/ibogaine-demerx-atai-mhra-clinical-trial/)].
- **State funding.** Texas SB 2308 (2025) established a $50M public-private grant program for ibogaine IND-enabling trials for OUD and co-occurring SUDs, with the requirement that awardees pursue Breakthrough Therapy designation upon demonstration of clinical efficacy [[Texas Legislature, SB 2308 analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB02308I.htm)]. The April 2026 Executive Order adds a matching $50M federal allocation through ARPA-H [[Reason Foundation commentary](https://reason.org/commentary/trumps-executive-order-accelerate-widespread-access-psychedelic-therapies/)].
- **Where human trials and treatments currently occur.** Brazil (unregulated, physician-prescribed importation; active clinical research at UNIFESP/USP/UNICAMP); Mexico (unregulated, large treatment-center ecosystem including Ambio Life Sciences in Tijuana used by MISTIC); New Zealand (scheduled non-approved medicine, physician-prescribable since 2009); South Africa and Australia (prescribable in certain contexts); The Netherlands (historically, in Dutch research protocols including Knuijver 2024). All currently published human efficacy data come from outside the United States [[Rodríguez et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35012793/); [MAPS bulletin](https://maps.org/news/bulletin/the-case-of-brazil-after-ayahuasca-ibogaine-opens-up-opportunity-for-first-class-research-in-the-global-south/)].

### 5. PET Imaging of D2R After Ibogaine

No published PET imaging study in humans or non-human primates has measured striatal D2/D3 receptor availability (for example with [¹¹C]raclopride, [¹⁸F]fallypride, or [¹¹C]PHNO) before and after ibogaine exposure. The Stanford MISTIC program has reported EEG neurophysiology and cortical complexity changes, but neither that cohort nor any earlier Mash, Noller, Schenberg, or Knuijver dataset has released PET D2/D3 data [[Lissemore et al., 2025](https://www.usmedicine.com/clinical-topics/tbi/magnesium-ibogaine-therapy-could-offer-treatment-option-for-tbi/); [Cherian et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38182784/)]. Molecular imaging reviews of psychedelic compounds likewise do not identify any ibogaine D2 PET study [[Dyck & Wenzel, 2021, PMC8122807](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8122807/)]. **This is the single most consequential gap for integrating ibogaine into a D2-recovery framework**: the mechanistic case rests on rodent GDNF/BDNF data, not on human receptor-level endpoints. A [¹¹C]raclopride or [¹⁸F]fallypride arm in the next wave of Stanford or DemeRx trials would directly address whether the sustained clinical improvements are accompanied by restoration of striatal D2/D3 binding potential comparable to the 13.89% striatal D2/D3 increase reported by Robertson et al. 2016 for exercise.

## 9-Methyl-β-Carboline (9-Me-BC)

### 1. Mechanistic Profile

9-Me-BC (9-methylpyrido[3,4-b]indole; 9-methylnorharman; C12H10N2; MW 182.27) is a fully synthetic methylated derivative of the endogenous β-carboline norharmane, not known to occur in nature. It is produced by Eschweiler–Clarke N9-methylation of β-carboline [[Wikipedia, 9-Methyl-β-carboline, referencing Hamann 2008 and subsequent Dresden/Berlin literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-Methyl-%CE%B2-carboline)]. Its pharmacology was delineated almost entirely by a single research program led by Rommelspacher, Wernicke, Hamann, Gille, Polanski, Keller and colleagues (Charité Berlin and TU Dresden).

**Dopamine synthesis and TH upregulation.** In primary mesencephalic cultures from embryonic mice, 48 h treatment with 9-Me-BC (25–100 μM; optimal ~70–90 μM; toxic >125 μM) produced up to a ~33% increase in tyrosine hydroxylase-immunoreactive (TH+) neuron counts and increased TH, DAT, and ALDH1A transcript levels [[Hamann, Wernicke, Lehmann, Reichmann, Rommelspacher & Gille, 2008, Neurochem Int 52:688–700, doi:10.1016/j.neuint.2007.08.018, PubMed 17913302](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0197018607002483)]. Upregulated transcription factors relevant to dopaminergic maturation included Nurr1, Pitx3, En1, En2, Gata2, Gata3, Creb1, and Crebbp, together with developmental morphogens Shh, Wnt1, and Wnt5a [[Hamann et al., 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0197018607002483); [Polanski et al., 2010, J Neurochem 113:1659–1675, doi:10.1111/j.1471-4159.2010.06725.x, PubMed 20374418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20374418/)].

**VMAT2.** The Dresden/Berlin literature documents increased dopamine content and dopamine uptake capacity in treated cultures but does not report a direct, receptor-level VMAT2 upregulation assay for 9-Me-BC; the dopamine-content increase is attributed to combined TH stimulation and MAO inhibition rather than to demonstrated VMAT2 transcript or protein changes [[Hamann et al., 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0197018607002483); [Keller et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8592951/)]. This contrasts with noribogaine, where dual VMAT2/SERT inhibition has been explicitly characterized. Claims of VMAT2 upregulation by 9-Me-BC should therefore be treated with caution pending direct assay.

**MAO-A/B inhibition.** Using the MAO-Glo assay with human recombinant enzymes, 9-Me-BC inhibited MAO-A with IC50 = 1 μM and MAO-B with IC50 = 15.5 μM (~15-fold preference for MAO-A), consistent with the pattern seen for most β-carbolines [[Keller, Polanski, Enzensperger, Reichmann, Hermann & Gille, 2020, J Neural Transm 127:999–1012, doi:10.1007/s00702-020-02189-9, PubMed 32285253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32285253/); [PMC8592951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8592951/)]. For reference, clinical rasagiline has IC50 412 nM (MAO-A) and 4.43 nM (MAO-B); 9-Me-BC is therefore a relatively weak MAO inhibitor overall, but sufficient at cellular concentrations to contribute to elevated intracellular dopamine.

**Neurotrophic induction.** In both primary midbrain cultures and cortical astrocyte cultures, 9-Me-BC induced transcription of a broad neurotrophic panel: BDNF (≈2-fold), GDNF, CDNF, NGF, CNTF, neurturin (Nrtn), artemin (Artn; ≈3.2-fold), persephin (Pspn), TGF-β2, NCAM1, and neurotrophin-3 [[Polanski et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20374418/); [Keller et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32285253/)]. Effects are mediated through the PI3K/Akt pathway — demonstrated by complete abrogation of TH+ neuron stimulation by the PI3K inhibitor LY-294002 — and uptake of 9-Me-BC occurs through the dopamine transporter in neurons and through an organic cation transporter (OCT) in astrocytes [[Keller et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8592951/); [Polanski, Reichmann & Gille, 2011, Expert Rev Neurother 11:845–860, doi:10.1586/ern.11.1, PubMed 21651332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21651332/)].

**In vivo restoration in rodents.** Wernicke et al. administered 9-Me-BC (0.105 mg/kg/day, 14 days, ICV) to rats in which MPP⁺ had been infused for 28 days to reduce striatal dopamine by ~50%. 9-Me-BC normalized striatal dopamine, restored TH-immunoreactive neuron counts in the substantia nigra, increased activity of respiratory chain complex I by ~80%, and induced BDNF, CDNF (conserved DA neurotrophic factor), cerebellin 1, and CNTF at the transcript and protein level [[Wernicke et al., 2010, Pharmacol Rep 62:35–53, doi:10.1016/S1734-1140(10)70241-3, PubMed 20360614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20360614/?dopt=Abstract); [Springer link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1016/S1734-1140(10)70241-3)]. In healthy rats, 10 days (but not 5 days) of 0.19 mg/kg/day s.c. 9-Me-BC improved radial-maze spatial learning, elevated hippocampal dopamine, and increased dendritic complexity and spine density on dentate granule cells [[Gruss et al., 2012, J Neurochem 121:924–931, doi:10.1111/j.1471-4159.2012.07713.x, PubMed 22380576](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22380576/)]. Additional actions relevant to dopaminergic restoration include inhibition of microglial proliferation, reduction of inflammatory cytokine expression, and a lowering of cellular α-synuclein content [[Polanski et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20374418/); [Polanski, Reichmann & Gille, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21651332/)].

### 2. Evidence (or Absence) of D2R Upregulation in Striatum

This is a critical point: **no experiment in the Wernicke/Hamann/Polanski/Keller corpus has demonstrated an increase in striatal D2 (DRD2) or D3 receptor density, transcript, or binding potential after 9-Me-BC**. On the contrary, a load-bearing control experiment in Keller et al. 2020 specifically tested whether the D2/D3 antagonist sulpiride (20 μM, pre-treated) altered the 9-Me-BC–induced increase in TH+ neurons: co-treatment did not change the stimulatory effect, leading the authors to conclude that "the stimulatory effects of 9-me-BC are dopamine receptor independent" [[Keller et al., 2020, PMC8592951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8592951/)]. This conclusion is echoed in secondary literature [[Wikipedia, citing Keller 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-Methyl-%CE%B2-carboline)]. The Dresden group's mechanistic model is that 9-Me-BC restores dopaminergic *cellular* phenotype (TH+ neuron number and neurite complexity, mitochondrial bioenergetics, neurotrophic milieu) and *pre-synaptic* dopamine content, not that it reverses post-synaptic D2/D3 downregulation. Consequently, the theoretical rationale for including 9-Me-BC in a D2-recovery framework is analogous to that for ibogaine — relief of hypodopaminergic tone should, over time, remove the drive sustaining ΔFosB/HDAC1-mediated DRD2 repression — but it is not supported by any direct D2R endpoint.

### 3. Human Trial Data

**There are no published human clinical trials of 9-Me-BC.** Systematic searches of PubMed, Embase, and ClinicalTrials.gov yield no registered or completed studies of safety, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, or efficacy in humans [[Wikipedia, 9-Me-BC, noting absence of human studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-Methyl-%CE%B2-carboline); [Holistic Nootropics substance review, 2024](https://holisticnootropics.com/substances/9-methyl-carboline/)]. The "German small trials" phrase sometimes used informally in nootropic communities refers to the preclinical rodent work of the Dresden/Berlin group (Wernicke 2010; Gruss 2012; Polanski 2010; Keller 2020), not to human studies. Informal self-experimentation reports in nootropic forums describe oral doses of 15–30 mg/day with 2–4 week cycles, scaled loosely from the Gruss 2012 rat subcutaneous dose (0.19 mg/kg/day); these are not clinical data and have no safety validation [[Holistic Nootropics, 2024](https://holisticnootropics.com/substances/9-methyl-carboline/); [Nootropicology review](https://nootropicology.com/9-me-bc/)]. Any scientific document should state plainly: **the entire evidence base for 9-Me-BC is preclinical (in vitro and rodent in vivo).**

### 4. Regulatory and Legal Status

9-Me-BC is not scheduled in the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Canada, or Australia; it is not an FDA-approved drug, has no IND, and has not received any Breakthrough Therapy designation. It is sold by research-chemical suppliers as a "not for human consumption" laboratory reagent. Because it is a synthetic analogue, it is not captured by controls on its parent compound norharmane or on the related harmala alkaloids [[Wikipedia, 9-Me-BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-Methyl-%CE%B2-carboline)]. Its unregulated status is a structural consequence of the absence of human pharmacology rather than a positive safety judgement. Known preclinical safety concerns — most prominently UVA-driven photosensitization/DNA damage characteristic of 9-methylated β-carbolines, demonstrated for 9-methyl-norharmane, 9-methyl-harmane, and 9-methyl-harmine (and by extension likely relevant to 9-Me-BC) — argue against treating unregulated status as equivalent to demonstrated safety [[Moreno et al., 2013, Org Biomol Chem, on 9-methyl-β-carbolines as photosensitizers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5932301_9-Methyl-beta-carboline_up-regulates_the_appearance_of_differentiated_dopaminergic_neurones_in_primary_mesencephalic_culture); [Holistic Nootropics, 2024](https://holisticnootropics.com/substances/9-methyl-carboline/)].

### 5. Relation to Harmine, Harmaline, and Distinction from Norharmane/Harmane

The β-carboline family spans a very wide pharmacological range, and 9-Me-BC's profile differs substantively from its better-known relatives:

- **Norharmane (β-carboline, unsubstituted)** is the parent compound of 9-Me-BC; endogenously present, a GABA_A inverse agonist at benzodiazepine sites, and in brain it is enzymatically converted by N-methyltransferases to 2-methyl-β-carbolinium and then to **2,9-dimethyl-β-carbolinium (2,9-diMe-BC⁺), which is a potent MPP⁺-like dopaminergic neurotoxin** implicated in Parkinson's disease pathogenesis [[Pavlovic et al., 2006, Neuroscience, PubMed 16517085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16517085/); [Polanski, Reichmann & Gille, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21651332/)]. The structural similarity between neuroprotective 9-Me-BC and toxic 2,9-diMe-BC⁺ (both bearing N9 methylation; the latter additionally carrying N2 methylation and a permanent positive charge) is one reason the Dresden group emphasizes that conclusions about 9-Me-BC cannot be extrapolated from other β-carbolines.
- **Harman (1-methyl-β-carboline)** is a nanomolar MAO-A inhibitor with pro-dopaminergic actions and is elevated in essential tremor and in tobacco smokers; it shares MAO-A inhibition with 9-Me-BC but lacks the TH-stimulatory / neurotrophic / PI3K-Akt profile [[Polanski, Reichmann & Gille, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21651332/)].
- **Harmine (7-methoxy-1-methyl-β-carboline)** and **harmaline (dihydro-harmine)**, the principal ayahuasca/Peganum harmala alkaloids, are potent, largely reversible MAO-A inhibitors and DYRK1A inhibitors, with documented effects on β-cell proliferation and tau phosphorylation but without the selective TH-stimulatory or α-synuclein-lowering actions of 9-Me-BC [[Li et al., 2017, Fitoterapia](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0944711319301357); [Patel et al., 2012, Asian Pac J Trop Biomed, PMC3609365](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3609365/)]. Harmaline at high doses produces cerebellar Purkinje-cell toxicity — a finding also observed for high-dose ibogaine in rats though not confirmed in primates [[Wikipedia, Ibogaine safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibogaine)].
- **9-methyl-harmine, 9-methyl-harmane, and 9-methyl-norharmane** are distinct compounds sharing N9 methylation. They are not equivalent to 9-Me-BC, and studies of their UVA photosensitization and DNA-damage properties (single-strand breaks, oxidized purines, CPD formation) are the primary source of the photosensitivity concern that extends by analogy to 9-Me-BC [[Moreno et al., cited in β-carboline literature](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5932301_9-Methyl-beta-carboline_up-regulates_the_appearance_of_differentiated_dopaminergic_neurones_in_primary_mesencephalic_culture)].

The key distinction to preserve in any synthesis is that N9 methylation of β-carboline produces a neuroprotective, neuritogenic, MAO-A-inhibiting compound (9-Me-BC), whereas additional N2 methylation produces the neurotoxic β-carbolinium cation (2,9-diMe-BC⁺) — a single methyl group distinguishes the restorative candidate from a Parkinsonian toxin.

## Integration Summary for the Target Document

For the D2-recovery framework already assembled in the host document, ibogaine and 9-Me-BC sit in distinct epistemic categories:

- **Ibogaine** has the most plausible human-translational case among pharmacological D2-relevant candidates — grounded in VTA GDNF induction with an autoregulatory persistence mechanism that is mechanistically complementary to the ΔFosB/HDAC1 repression axis the host document describes, and supported by stimulant-specific open-label data from Brazil (Schenberg 2014; 61% abstinence, median 5.5–8.4 months) and combined opioid/cocaine data from St. Kitts (Mash 2018; N = 191). However, the case is built from rodent GDNF studies; no PET D2/D3 imaging has been performed after ibogaine in humans or primates, and QTc prolongation averaging 95 ms with documented Torsades de Pointes requires CYP2D6-aware dosing, magnesium supplementation, and extended telemetry. As of April 2026, ibogaine remains Schedule I in the US with the first IND cleared for noribogaine (DMX-1001, DemeRx/atai, AUD) and the first Executive-Order-driven federal funding mechanism ($50M + Texas $50M); no FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation has yet been granted for ibogaine itself.
- **9-Me-BC** has an attractive preclinical profile — TH upregulation, PI3K/Akt-mediated neuritogenesis, BDNF/GDNF/CDNF induction, MAO-A/B inhibition (IC50 1 / 15.5 μM), microglial suppression, α-synuclein reduction, and in vivo reversal of MPP⁺-induced striatal dopamine loss — but it has never been tested in humans, its stimulatory actions on TH+ neurons are explicitly sulpiride-insensitive (i.e., D2/D3-independent), and N9-methylated β-carbolines carry documented UVA phototoxicity liability. For the host document, 9-Me-BC is best framed as a preclinical tool compound illustrating the feasibility of pharmacologically restoring dopaminergic cellular phenotype and neurotrophic milieu — one methyl group away from 2,9-diMe-BC⁺, a Parkinsonian neurotoxin — rather than as a clinically actionable intervention.

The largest methodological gap common to both compounds, and the most productive next experiment in each case, is the same: a pre-/post-treatment [¹¹C]raclopride or [¹⁸F]fallypride PET study in chronic stimulant users, paralleling the Robertson 2016 exercise paradigm (13.89% striatal D2/D3 BPND increase). Until such data exist, inclusion of ibogaine or 9-Me-BC in a D2-recovery framework should be explicitly qualified as *mechanism-derived hypothesis* rather than *demonstrated human D2R upregulation*.
